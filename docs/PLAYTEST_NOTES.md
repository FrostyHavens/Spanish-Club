# Playtest notes

Real players' reactions, newest first. Kept so design decisions can point back to what children actually said.

## 2026-10-09 — a 4-year-old (younger than the target age; useful for the age range)
Played on iPad. In their words: "I like that you get to touch the screen and don't have to press any buttons. I like
the pet animal. I didn't like how it didn't let me go in the door that was locked, I never got to go in there. I liked
waiting for night and day to start again. I liked finding the cat on the post, that was exciting, I tried really hard
to find it and it was so cool. I liked the touch screen feature a lot, it made me happy. I like that you have your own
house."

What it suggests:
- **Tap-to-walk works** even at 4; no buttons is a big plus.
- **Hide-and-seek finds are the highlight** (the cat on the fence post): more find-it moments with animals hidden in
  the world.
- **A closed door is a disappointment**: the barn (sign over its door, no way in) reads as "locked". Doors that look
  enterable should open, with something to do inside, or clearly show why not yet.
- **The day/night cycle and having your own house** are appealing: home could be a place to decorate or collect in.
- **The pet** remains the emotional anchor.
