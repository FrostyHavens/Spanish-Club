# Club de Español

A 16-bit-style educational game for kids aged about 7 to 12 who are learning Spanish. There is no combat. You explore an open world (no locks, no bosses): the sunny town of **Villa Sol** in the middle, and around it **La playa**, **El bosque**, **El parque de juegos**, **La casa de los abuelos** and **La montaña**, reached on foot along paths or by bus. You talk with the people you meet in Spanish, help them, find animals for your album, and play with your dog (you name him). Every new word you meet becomes a picture card that grows (a seed, a sprout, a flower) as you use it; later a notebook organizes them.

It uses the same hand-drawn-in-code engine as *Embers of Aldmere*: pixel tiles, chibi map sprites, anime portraits and the FM-synth music.

## Play
- **Play online: https://frostyhavens.github.io/Spanish-Club/** (updates automatically when `main` changes).
- Open `dist/club-de-espanol.html` in any modern browser. It's a single file and works offline.
- Or open `index.html`, which loads the scripts from `src/`. Use this one when editing.

**Controls**
| Key | Action |
| --- | --- |
| Arrows / WASD | walk, choose |
| Z / Enter / Space | talk, search, OK |
| X / Esc | menu, back |
| C | hear the line again |
| M | sound on/off |
| V | the mic: say the answer out loud |
| hold Space | talk while the mic button is showing: hold, say it, let go (otherwise Space is OK) |

On phones and tablets, just tap: tap a spot to walk there, tap people to talk to them (and doors, signs or a sparkle to go or look there), tap a picture card to answer, tap the speaker to hear a line again, and the deck of word cards in the corner opens the menu. Every screen can be played with taps or with the keys.

**Saving is automatic.** The game saves by itself all the time (new words, pages, errands, every finished conversation, a new map, every 15 seconds and when the tablet goes to sleep). Tap the title to play: the first time it goes straight to making a character; after that *¿Quién juega?* shows three save slots (face, name, word cards). Tap a card to continue, or *+ Nuevo* to start another child's game. To delete a slot, hold its little trash can for 3 seconds, then choose ✓. The saves live only in this browser (Safari clears a site after 7 days without a visit, and "Clear History" wipes it), so the grown-ups menu has **Back up / restore** (below). Grown-ups see whether it is saving ("Saved just now" / "Not saved since 10:42 (storage full)") there and on the Learning page; if saves keep failing, a small grey cloud shows under the map's menu button until one works again. A slot that can't be read shows as empty; before a new game is written there, its text is kept (`spanishclub_slotN_bad`) and goes into backups.

**Grown-ups menu:** hold the gear for 2 seconds (in the map's menu next to the word cards and the album, or on the title; a ring fills while you hold; on a keyboard, move onto it and hold Z). It has Music / Sounds / Voice volume, Choose voice, English help, on-screen buttons (a D-pad with A/B/C, off unless you turn it on; remembered per device), **Speaking (mic)** (on by default where the browser can listen; greyed *not available* where it can't; remembered per device; off = no mic buttons anywhere and the game plays exactly as before), a microphone test (tap a word, tap the mic, say it, and see everything the device heard and whether it would pass; *Share log* opens the iPad share sheet, or *Copy log* copies it), controls and tips, **Learning** (what has stuck: days and minutes played, words met / known / remembered / solid, **retention** = right on the first try when a word comes back after a day or more, the share said aloud, every word by place with its strength and when it was last seen, slipping words, a 10-word check to ask away from the game, *tonight's question* for dinner, and *Share* as text), Story progress, **Back up / restore** (*Back up* makes one file with every slot and the settings, `club-de-espanol-<names>-<date>.json`: on the iPad the share sheet opens with it (Save to Files, AirDrop, Mail, Messages), on a computer it downloads; *Show as text* gives the same to copy into a note; *Restore from a file* or *Paste text* checks it, shows what's in it next to what's on this device, and replaces all three slots after *Yes, restore*, keeping what was there for *Undo restore*; this is also how a game moves between the iPad and a computer. It says whether the browser promised to keep the data, and when the last backup was: the Learning page asks for one after 7 days, and before a trip or a break), **Problem log** (errors the game caught and got past, the last 20: *Share* / *Copy*, *Clear*), and Back to title. There is no Save button: saving is automatic.

## How it teaches (like *Tunic*: you start knowing almost nothing)
- **No translations.** Characters speak short, simple Spanish. Meaning comes from pictures, context and what people do. (Grown-ups can turn on *English help* in the grown-ups menu.)
- **Picture and word, always together.** Every word in a sentence has its picture beside it, so they're seen together every time (🍎 *manzana*). A word you haven't met yet is its picture and a faint blank (like *Tunic*'s unreadable text: it can't be read before its puzzle; the voice still says it); once met the word is blue, and once you remember it, **gold**. In a question, a choice that's also in the question is heard (a little speaker), not shown; the right card, once picked, shows its picture and its word (`src/ui.js`, `src/words.js`, `docs/LEARNING_DESIGN.md`).
- **Word cards that grow** (`src/cards.js`; stars meant nothing to a 7-year-old, `docs/PLAYTEST_NOTES.md`). Every word met is a card: its picture, its word and a little plant in a pot. Each time the word is used, its card pops up right there and the plant grows before your eyes with a rising sound, then the card flies into the deck in the corner: picking it right (+2), finding it (+2), carrying or giving it, tapping it (+1, once in a while), and **saying it out loud grows it the most** (+5, with sound waves). Seed → sprout → leaves → bud → flower; it flowers only once the word is remembered (the card turns gold), and a solid word grows more flowers, the last one gold. The map's corner button is the deck (the number of cards beside it); the kid menu's first picture opens it: all the cards, newest first, tap one to hear it. No stars are shown to the child any more (they're still counted for the diploma).
- **Cards first, the book later.** Early on there are just cards. Once 15 words are met, a small celebration (a book drops in, cards fly into it, confetti) opens the notebook in the kid menu, as the organizer of the cards.
- **A ghost hand shows each new thing once** (`src/demo.js`). The first time a question's picture cards, a mic, Canelo, a thing to pick up, giving to Canelo, putting something down (tap yourself), a page puzzle or a hide-and-seek animal come up, a see-through hand does it once, slowly (the mic: a mouth talking beside it), without words and never showing an answer. The child's own tap stops it.
- **Learning by doing.** A word is *met* in a little puzzle with one unknown (someone holds a thing up and names it, then *¿Qué es?* among words you know; watch it happen then do it; hear it and point; find the thing someone needs), and its new card pops up and flies into the deck. Then it comes back: a minute or two later, later that day, the next day, two days later... (a small Leitner system), each time in a harder way: picture and word cards, then word cards under its picture, then listening for it or saying it. Remembering it turns it gold, with a big *¡Palabra de oro!* card.
- **Gentle mistakes.** A wrong choice wobbles and greys out with a soft boop, with no lecture, so every question can be finished. A right answer pops with a chime, and the word's card pops up beside it and grows.
- **A notebook that organizes the cards** (once it has opened). Each word is written in the moment it's met, and a topic's page appears with its first word. Words are blue, or gold once remembered, with their card's plant; tap one to hear it. The sparkles around town are **page puzzles** (before the book too): match 4 pictures of a page's words with their words (tap a picture then its word, or drag); each match grows that word's card. A sparkle waits until you've met 4 words of its page, and comes back on a later day when its words are due again.
- **Requests in pictures.** People who need something show it in a thought bubble (🍎🍎🍎 🍌🍌, a red ball, a letter going to the bakery). New errands appear as a picture card. Doors have picture signs. *Misiones* (the chapter going on, its steps, the badges) is in the grown-ups menu (*Story progress*): children don't go into menus to find out what to do next.
- **Make your character.** A new game starts by choosing **niño** or **niña**, then skin tone, hairstyle (6), hair color and outfit color, with a live preview of the walking sprite and portrait. A dice button picks a random look. Then **type your name** (keyboard, or the on-screen letter grid with Ñ and accents for touch screens); characters call you by it.
- **Spanish that matches you.** Townsfolk use the boy or girl form of words for the character you chose: *¡Bienvenido!* or *¡Bienvenida!*, *¡Qué listo!* or *¡Qué lista!*, *Eres un gran cartero* or *Eres una gran cartera*.
- **Spoken Spanish.** Lines and words are read aloud with a North American voice when the device has one (Mexico first, then the US, then other Latin American voices). Press **C** to hear the current line again. *Choose voice* in the grown-ups menu picks a different voice.
- **Separate volumes.** The grown-ups menu has sliders (0–10) for **Music**, **Sounds** and **Voice**: tap a level or slide a finger along it (left/right on a keyboard); Voice at 0 turns speech off. They're saved on the device, separately from game saves, like the voice, English help and on-screen buttons. M still mutes everything.
- **Speaking grows the cards most.** Questions whose answer is a word have a big pink mic button in the bottom-right corner. Tap it (or press V, or hold Space while you talk and let go when done), and it turns blue and listens (green once it hears a voice); say the answer. Saying the right one counts as choosing it, and its word card grows the most: the card glows blue, *¡Bien dicho!*, a sparkly sound, and the word's card pops up with sound waves around it and grows much more than for a tap. Saying one of the other choices is just like tapping it (it greys out, gently). Nothing heard, or a word that isn't one of the choices: a listening ear and *¡Otra vez!*, with no penalty. Close is good enough (the same matching as the mic test), and there is no grammar checking. Tapping always works, even while it listens, so speaking never blocks anything. The gold *¡Palabra de oro!* card has the mic too: say the word to grow it once more. The Cuaderno shows a little mic on words said out loud, and the diploma counts them. If the device blocks the mic (or Dictation is off), the mic buttons disappear for the rest of the session; turning *Speaking (mic)* on again in the grown-ups menu brings them back.
- **Say it to get it: phrases** (`src/phrases.js`, `docs/LEARNING_PLAN_2.md`). Six sentence patterns with a slot for a word she knows: *Me llamo ___* (meeting a friend, who says their name first), *Quiero ___* (the shops, ice cream, cookies, hot chocolate, pie), *___, por favor* (a bonus on any request: a heart), *¿Dónde está ___?* (Nico's hide-and-seek hint), *Me gusta ___* (Lola asks her favourite animal: a heart on it in the album) and *Tengo ___* (giving a present or a letter). Each is introduced once, the first time it comes up anywhere: two friends say it in a little comic strip, then *¡Ahora tú!*. She says it to the big mic and it happens; saying only the key word works too, then her speech bubble shows the whole sentence (frame and word, as pictures) and invites her to say it all for a bigger boost. Or tap the frame card and a word card to build it (read aloud). Phrases have their own cards (a speech-bubble mark) that grow like words, a *Frases* tab in the deck, a notebook page, and a tab on the grown-ups' Learning page.
- **Listen and do** (`src/listen.js`, `src/requests.js`, `docs/LEARNING_PLAN_2.md`). A friend asks for something out loud, and the request is written with **no pictures** for its words: *Dame tres conchas*, *Dame la manzana roja*, *Toca el perro grande*, *¿Cuántas conchas hay?*, later two details at once (*Dame dos flores amarillas*). Every request is made only from words she already knows, in correct Mexican Spanish (articles, plurals, colours that agree, *una concha*). A tray of pictures without labels: tap the right one, or count things into a basket and give it; for *¿Cuántas?* say the number to the mic or tap it. A speaker plays it again. Right: the friend lights up and the words' cards grow. Wrong: the right one glows and is said (a count is counted out loud), then one more try or go on: never stuck. One detail at a time until she gets several right first try, then two.
- **Always a next thing to do, in sight** (`src/guide.js`, `docs/INTUITIVE_AUDIT.md`). While you're free to walk, a yellow arrow bounces over the one next goal (the chapter's person, place, animal or door; home at sunset; a favour; Nico's hide-and-seek; Canelo's "?"). Off screen it waits at the screen's edge, pointing the way, with a little picture of who or what is there; in a building it's on the way out, in town on the right door. A puzzle's choices (who says *¡miau!*, which bush) all get a small white arrow, never only the answer. Stand about for a few seconds and Canelo trots a few tiles that way, barks and looks back. The person you're going to shows the chapter's picture in their bubble, never a bare "!".
- **Pick things up and carry them** (`src/carry.js`; a 7-year-old asked for it). Apples under the apple trees, sticks in the park, Canelo's blue ball in front of home, his bone by his cushion every morning, eggs in the barn's hay, flowers: tap one and you walk over, pick it up and hold it up over your head; its name is said with its picture (a "?" while its word isn't met: nothing new is taught this way). One kind at a time (a stack of three apples). Tap Canelo to throw the ball or the stick (he fetches it back to you) or give him the bone; tap someone who loves it (their bubble shows it) for a heart; anyone else says who would love it, with that friend's face. Tap yourself, or the thing in the corner, to put it down. Things come back after a while (the bone and the eggs every morning).
- **Never a dead end.** When the story has nothing for now (the next chapter opens in the morning, today's new words are used up, the evening chapter waits for sunset), the arrow points at the day's play: fetch, an apple for Profesora Luna, a flower for Mamá, the bone for Canelo, an egg for Rosa, bread for the ducks, a page puzzle; when those are done, the next goal turns: a trick for Canelo, petting him, water or a cookie for him, another round of hide-and-seek with Nico, the ball for Nico, a game of fetch (never endless fetch). The person whose chapter comes next sleeps under a moon bubble with "z z" floating up (or shows the moon alone: tonight). At sunset the arrow points home, and at home to your bed: a tap on it and it's good night.
- **A hand shows where to tap.** No controls screen: if a child does nothing for a few seconds, a little hand taps where to go next (the puppy's hiding place, the door, the nearest person with a bubble, a line waiting to go on). Any touch or key hides it. On a keyboard, a strip shows Z / X / C once after chapter 1.
- **The end of the day.** After about 18 minutes of play the sun sets over Villa Sol and a moon bubble floats over home. Going home then: Mamá says *¡Buenas noches!*, a *Hoy* card shows the cards that grew today, night falls, and a new morning starts with *¡Buenos días!* Nothing is forced; staying out to play is fine.

## The open world (`docs/WORLD_PLAN.md`, `src/zones.js`)
- **The opener**: Mamá says *hola*, a puppy bursts in and you **name him** (his name is already typed: *Canelo*; keep
  it with one tap, roll the dice, or type one). Then two big pictures: **play with the puppy** (chapter 1) or **go
  explore** (the world is open; his games wait at home with Mamá). His name is used everywhere.
- **Five places around Villa Sol**, each down a road from an edge of town (a signpost shows its picture) along a short
  path with animals and things to pick up. Any of them can be visited first. Arriving somewhere new is a little party
  (*¡Nuevo lugar!*). All five are built; each has its own story, people, eight-ish animals for the album and things
  for the dog to do.
- **La playa** (built): a beach with waves, a pier and a boat, rocks and a tide pool; Don Paco the fisherman, Marina the
  lifeguard, Doña Nieves's ice creams and Tere's sandcastles; seven sea animals for the album (a dolphin in the morning
  and an octopus at night are rare); a story about a lost net, a hot day and baby turtles; shells to collect, a castle
  to build, a little crab looking for a shell; the dog digs, splashes and chases the gulls.
- **El bosque** (built): tall trees, a waterfall into a pond, a river with a bridge and stepping stones, the bears'
  cave (go inside: glowing mushrooms, fireflies, mama bear asleep) and Doña Lupe's log cabin; Elena the forest ranger;
  eight woodland animals for the album (squirrels that run up the trees, deer that bound away; an owl at night and the
  bear in the cave are rare); a story about a lost bear cub (big and small), a squirrel's acorns and a raccoon who took
  the ranger's binoculars; blackberries for a pie, a big-or-small mushroom game, leaves for Lola, fireflies in a jar at
  night; the dog sniffs the hollow log, follows a trail of paw prints and swims across the river for a stick.
- **El parque de juegos** (built): a park with a playground (swings, a slide, a climbing frame, a seesaw, a
  merry-go-round, a sandbox), a kite hill, a little soccer field, a pond and a garden with a fountain; Ana, Pablo and Don
  Chuy the bubble man. Here the child SAYS an action word (*¡corre!, ¡salta!, ¡sube!, ¡baja!, ¡empuja!, ¡lanza!,
  ¡atrapa!, ¡baila!...*) or taps it, and her character, the kids and the dog DO it (tap yourself: *¿Qué hago?*); every
  ride is tappable; eight park critters for the album (marching ants, a cricket at night and a hummingbird in the morning
  are rare); a story of Simón dice, Pablo's kite stuck in a tree (climb up, then run fast to fly it) and the whole
  playground; a jump rope, a ball game, bubbles to catch (and a bubble wand); the dog digs up a ball, runs his jumping
  course and chases the pigeons.
- **La casa de los abuelos** (built): the grandparents' countryside home: a cottage you go into (the family photos, the
  fireplace and the baby cousin in the living room, Abuela's kitchen with a mouse, the bedroom with the wardrobe, the
  mirror and the dressing-up trunk), an orchard with a swing, the henhouse, the vegetable garden and the water pump,
  the washing on the clothesline, a duck pond, cows and a donkey, sheep, turkeys and pigs in the mud. Family, the body
  and clothes: what you put on (*la camisa, el pantalón, el vestido, los zapatos, el sombrero...*) your character
  WEARS, on every map; *¡toca tu nariz!* and your character touches it (tap yourself: *¿Qué toco?*). Eight animals
  for the album (a rooster at dawn and a possum that plays dead at night are rare); a story of getting ready for the
  family photo (clothes off the line and a sock the dog runs off with; washing off the pigs' mud; shoes, a hat, the
  baby who crawled off to the ducks; everyone on the porch: *¡whisky!*); cookies with Abuela, Abuelo's garden, Simón
  dice, a fashion show, Abuelo's jacket at night, the photo wall; the dog digs up a carrot, steals a sock (a tug of war)
  and chases the turkeys.
- **La montaña** (built, the last zone): a green meadow under snowy peaks: Mateo's weather station (its vane spins in
  the wind), Don Lucho's refuge under a snowy roof (go in for hot chocolate), the llamas' corral, firs full of monarch
  butterflies; up the hill the snow: a sledding slope, a snowfield for a snowman, the marmots' rocks, a frozen pond to
  skate on and a lookout with binoculars (the view over the clouds). The weather CHANGES: it snows, rains, blows, clouds
  float over, the sun shines; at the weather station the child SAYS the weather she wants and it comes (after rain, the
  sun brings a rainbow). Up in the snow you shiver without a *gorro*, a *bufanda* or *guantes* (worn everywhere after).
  Eight animals for the album (an eagle only in the sunshine and a wolf howling at night are rare); a story of the cold,
  a snowman (the dog steals his carrot nose) and the rainbow; a sled race with the dog (he pulls you back up), skating,
  animal tracks in the snow, hot chocolate (the dog gets a bone), *¿Qué tiempo hace?*; the dog rolls in the snow, digs a
  snow tunnel and chases the marmots.
- **The bus**: every place has a bus stop. Tap it: the world map opens (the places you've been to in colour, the others
  as "?"); tap a place and the bus comes, you hop on with your dog, and it drops you there. The map in the kid menu
  works too (the bus comes to you). Tapping a "?" shows footprints and the arrow suggests the way on foot.
- **The world is connected** (`src/connect.js`): **Tomás the mail carrier** has a letter every day (his bubble shows
  it) for someone in a place you've been to: he holds it up with their face and their place's picture; the letter in
  your hands shows their face, the arrow leads to the bus stop (the world map shows the letter over that place) and
  then to them; they open it (*¡Es de Mamá!*), say thank you, and you get a heart and a star; every other letter they
  write back, so letters go both ways. Some days Tomás is on his round at a place's bus stop. **Everyone has hearts**:
  the town's twelve and the places' people (Don Paco, Marina, Doña Nieves, Tere, Elena, Doña Lupe, Ana, Pablo, Don
  Chuy, Abuela, Abuelo, the baby, Don Lucho, Mateo) greet you each day once you know them (say it back; then one word
  of their place), love a present (a shell, a leaf, an acorn, the ball, an apple, a cookie, bread, an ice cream, a
  flower), tell a secret at 3 hearts (a rare animal of their place, in pictures) with a sticker, and give a photo at 5.
  **Lola** gets a heart the first time you meet her in each place: explore all five and she's your best friend.
- **Friends in every place**: Lola, the friend who travels; Nico's hide-and-seek (a game in every place); your dog
  always has something to do in every place (dig in the sand, splash in the waves, sniff a log, follow a trail, run his
  jumping course, steal a sock, roll in the snow, dig a snow tunnel), and comes along on every path and on the bus.
- **The whole world's progress**: each place earns three stars (its story, its album page, its friends), shown over it
  on the world map; the map's **trophy** opens *¡Mi mundo!* (places visited, words, animals, friends; a card per place
  with its stars, chapters, album and hearts), also the diploma's second page. A place whose story and album page are
  complete gets a *¡Completo!* celebration. The **Amigos** page has a tab per place (faces, hearts, stickers; Lola's
  card shows where you've met her). The grown-ups' *Story progress* has a second page with every place's chapters.
- **The album is a Pokédex**: a page per place, numbered animals, silhouettes with a picture of where they live, rare
  ones that only come out at night or in the morning, *¡Completo!* per place, and *¡Nuevo animal!* the first time.
- **No daily word limit**: nothing waits on a word count; good teaching lives inside each activity.

## The story of Villa Sol in chapters (plan: `docs/CURRICULUM.md`)
The game is one story in 21 short chapters, played in order over about three weeks of 15-minute sessions. Each brings
0-5 new words, each met in a little puzzle with one unknown and used again within a few minutes; most of every later
chapter is older words coming back in the things people need. A new chapter opens when the last is done (no daily word
limit any more; morning and evening chapters wait for their time of day, their giver asleep under a moon meanwhile).
It is Villa Sol's quest line: never required to leave town or to explore; every zone can have its own line. Misiones (grown-ups menu) shows the chapter going on with its steps, or the next. When a new word hasn't come
back by itself a minute later, Canelo shows a "?": tap him and say or pick it.
1. **¡Un perro!** Mamá says *hola*; a puppy bursts through the door, hides under the table, barks *guau*, and comes when you say *¡ven!*: Canelo is yours.
2. **El gato de la cerca.** Canelo chases a butterfly to the park; Nico, his cat (*gato*, *miau*) and a sound game.
3. **¡Buenos días, Canelo!** *buenos días*, a bone (*hueso*) and Canelo learns *¡siéntate!*
4. **El juego de Don Pepe.** *sí* and *no* about Canelo and the cat, and an apple (*manzana*) for Abuela Rosa.
5. **La pelota roja.** Sofía's ball (*pelota*); the red one (*rojo*) is lost in the bushes.
6. **Pan para los patos.** Bread (*pan*), *gracias*, and feeding the ducks (*pato*, *cuac*) with Nico.
7. **¿Dónde está Canelo?** He runs off; the neighbours point the way: the park, a bench, the fountain, the farm, and a goat.
8. **La escuela de Luna.** Profesora Luna's school: ask three friends *¿cómo estás?* — *bien*.
9. **¡Buenas noches, Canelo!** The first evening: water (*agua*), his bed (*cama*), *buenas noches*.
10. **Tomás está cansado.** The tired mailman: three letters (*carta*), Rosa's house (*casa*), a horse (*caballo*) eats one, *adiós*.
11. **Los huevos de Rosa.** Her hens (*gallina*), eggs (*huevo*), a nest with *uno* and one with *dos*, the white hen (*blanco*); a hen runs off and comes back on *¡ven!*
12. **El mercado de Mamá.** Mamá's list at Don Pepe's: bananas and oranges (*plátano*, *naranja*), *por favor*, *tres*, *cuatro*.
13. **El día de campo.** Rosa's picnic: the bakery (*panadería*), cheese (*queso*), milk from the goat (*leche*), five things in the basket (*cinco*), six plates (*seis*) on a blanket in the park.
14. **El show de perros.** Sofía teaches Canelo *¡dame la pata!* and *¡salta!*; cookies (*galleta*); Luna judges from picture signs; a blue ribbon (*azul*); Sofía is *feliz*.
15. **Las flores de Lucía.** Lucía is *triste*: a pink and a yellow flower (*flor*, *rosado*, *amarillo*) for her mom, a butterfly (*mariposa*) on Canelo's nose.
16. **Las páginas perdidas.** The wind blows your notebook's cards into the park; Canelo learns *¡busca!* and sniffs them out; one is up a tree (*árbol*), a rabbit (*conejo*) sits on the last; Inés's library (*biblioteca*).
17. **¿Qué dicen?** Nico's sound game around town: a green frog (*rana*, *croac*, *verde*), a bird (*pájaro*), and Canelo learns *¡gira!*
18. **¿Cuántos animales?** Count the town's animals with Luna: a fish (*pez*), *siete*, *ocho*.
19. **¡Todos a la granja!** Call every animal with *¡ven!*: they follow you to the farm in a parade; the horse and the goat make *nueve* and *diez*.
20. **Preparamos la fiesta.** Invitations, food, ribbons, flowers and a rehearsal all over town (no new words).
21. **La fiesta de los animales.** The party at the barn: Canelo's show, the animals' song, a group photo and the diploma.

## Every day in town: review in the world
- **The dog's warm-up**: the first time you're free to walk each day, the dog trots off and comes back with a word card in his mouth: 3-4 words from earlier days come back (say the word with the big mic beside its picture, or tap it); each card grows, he wags, and at the end he jumps for joy and gets a bone. Once she knows enough words, one of them is a request (*Dame dos huesos*, *Toca el pato grande*). About a minute; the two-arrow button skips it. A word missed three times is *slipping*: it is met again, like new, instead of asked the same way.
- **Greetings**: the first talk of the day with someone starts with their greeting (a sun, a moon or a wave: *buenos días*, *buenas noches*, *hola*), then one word that's due, asked the way their life shows it.
- **Favores**: up to three townsfolk a day have a "?" bubble and a small favour about a word you know: take a letter to a place named only by its word, find an animal, count a heap of their things, say a colour, a sound, a face, a thing (its card grows, and a heart).
- **Listen-and-do favours** (an ear in the bubble, at most two a day): Don Pepe, Nico, Doña Lupe, Elena, Abuelo and Mateo ask for two things on the tray (fruit by colour and number, animals big and small, berries and mushrooms, snowmen and mittens); Lola (shells on the beach, leaves in the forest, apples at the grandparents') and Lucía (flowers) ask for things to bring: they appear around them on the map, she picks up the right ones (*Tráeme dos conchas rosadas*) and brings them, for a star and a heart.
- **La palabra del día**: Profesora Luna holds up one picture a day: say its word.
- **Canelo**: six tricks, each from his chapter (*ven*, *siéntate*, *dame la pata*, *salta*, *busca*, *gira*); say them at his menu. His "?" brings back a word met a minute ago.
- **Page puzzles**: sparkles around town, and from chapter 16 Inés's library, match a notebook page's pictures with their words.
- **Side jobs** (once a day each): feed the ducks, the hens' egg for Rosa, Canelo's water, pet the horse, wake the sleepy cat; after chapter 15, a flower a day as a present. **Presents**: someone who likes what you carry shows it in their bubble.
- **¿Dónde están? (hide-and-seek)**: once a day Nico (a magnifying glass over his head) hides two or three animals you know around town and in the barn: on a fence post, on the bakery roof, behind your house, in the trees, behind hay bales and barrels. They peek out (an ear, a comb, a tail); tap one to find it: its card grows, its name (say it back!) and a question when it's due. Nico shows a picture of the place as a hint, and Canelo sniffs out a trail of paw prints.
- **The barn**: its door opens. Inside: the cream horse in her stall, hens on the perch over their nest boxes, hay bales that rustle, a trough, a milk can, apples, and the *¿Qué dicen?* page puzzle. Every door in town opens; one that can't just yet says why (Mamá: *¿Y el perro?*).
- **The animals and the album**, **hearts** with everyone (stickers at 3, a photo at 5), the **evening at home** with the *Hoy* card, and the **diploma** at the end.

## Content
180 words on 26 notebook pages and 6 phrases (`content/es/words.js`): 73 words taught by the 21 chapters of Villa Sol
(`content/es/story-c01-c10.js`, `content/es/story-c11-c21.js`), the rest by the five zones' people and places
(`content/es/zone-*.js`). Progress saves itself in the browser, in three save slots; saves from before the
chapters (and from part 1, when chapters 11-21 were still the older errands) load with their errands mapped onto the
chapters.
